Buying a used graphics card?
Test first, then pay.

How it works

Create the stick

Once, at home: download the image and write it to a USB stick (16 GB or larger) with Rufus (Windows) or balenaEtcher.

Boot at the seller's

Plug the stick into the PC that holds the card. While it powers on, open the boot menu (usually F8, F11 or F12) and pick the USB stick.

Run the test

After about a minute the desktop appears. Double-click “GPU-Schnelltest” (quick test) in the top left and wait 5 minutes.

Nothing is installed or changed on the seller's PC. Everything runs from the stick and no boot entries are created. Please don't change BIOS settings – see Secure Boot.

What gets checked

  • AuthenticityModel, memory size, BIOS – against fake or reflashed cards
  • Video memoryMemory test across the whole VRAM: faulty chips show up immediately
  • Compute errors under full loadThe card computes at full power; every wrong result counts
  • ArtifactsFull-screen 3D load – stripes or coloured dots are easy to spot
  • TemperaturesCore, hotspot and memory: reveal dried-out paste and worn thermal pads
  • Power, clocks, fansDoes the card reach its power limit? Do all fans spin?
  • Power delivery & slotPower-related throttling, PCIe link, driver error messages

FAQ

Do I need Linux skills?

No. The stick boots straight into a desktop with large icons. One double-click starts the test.

Which cards are supported?

NVIDIA GeForce RTX 30 (Ampere), RTX 40 (Ada) and RTX 50 (Blackwell). Not AMD or Intel. So far tested on real hardware: RTX 3090.

What do I need on site?

The stick, a monitor connected to the graphics card (not the motherboard) and a PC with a sufficient power supply. Connect every power plug of the card with its own cable.

What about Secure Boot? (important)

The stick boots with Secure Boot enabled – that's the normal way to test. All key checks run: memory test, compute errors, artifacts, core temperature, power, fans. Only hotspot and memory temperature show “SKIP” – that is not a bad sign.

Those two readings need Secure Boot disabled. Only the PC owner should decide that: if Windows uses BitLocker or device encryption (default on many new PCs), Windows will ask for the recovery key afterwards. Some games with anti-cheat also require Secure Boot – so turn it back on.

Easiest: the seller tests beforehand with Secure Boot disabled and posts the result in the listing.

What does the test not detect?

Coil whine (listen to the card), smell, visible damage and rare faults that only show up after hours. The test greatly lowers the risk of a bad purchase but is no guarantee.
